Placement model

The mixed-integer programme the planner solves (src/civ6_planner/model.py), with HiGHS through scipy.optimize.milp. Every symbol below is tied to the code that computes it, so this page and the code can be checked against each other. Defaults are those of config/default.toml.

1. Overview

Given the map the player has revealed, the model chooses

  • tiles on which to found new cities (by default as many as fit), and
  • for each new city and each of the player's existing cities, a tile for each planned district it does not have yet (Campus, Industrial Zone, Commercial Hub, Holy Site, Theater, Encampment, Harbor, Aqueduct, Dam),
  • for each city, up to five tiles to improve (Farm, Mine, Quarry, Plantation, Camp, Pasture, Fishing Boats, Lumber Mill),

so as to maximise the weighted yields of the city sites, the districts' adjacency bonuses and the improved (worked) tiles, minus the yields of the tiles the districts cover. The game's placement rules are enforced partly by which variables exist (sets \(C\) and \(R_{d,c}\), \(G_{i,c}\)) and partly by linear constraints (1)–(10).

The model is built in three steps, each a list of small functions in model.py: VARIABLES, CONSTRAINTS, OBJECTIVE. Commenting out a line drops that constraint or term; each run's log lists the ones that were on.

2. Map, distance, neighbourhoods

Tiles. \(T\) is the set of tiles in the map dump: the tiles revealed to the player (GameMap.tiles). A tile \(t\) has offset coordinates \((x_t, y_t)\), \(0\le x_t < W\), \(0\le y_t < H\), with \(y\) growing northwards and odd rows shifted half a hex east ("odd-r"). The map wraps east–west.
Hex distance. With axial coordinates \(q(x,y) = x - (y - (y \bmod 2))/2\) and \(r = y\): $$ \delta(t,u) \;=\; \min_{s\in\{-W,\,0,\,W\}} \tfrac12\Big(|q_t^{(s)}-q_u| + |y_t-y_u| + |q_t^{(s)}+y_t-q_u-y_u|\Big), \qquad q_t^{(s)} = q(x_t+s,\,y_t). $$ GameMap.distance; checked against the game's own neighbour lists (282/282 pairs).
Neighbourhoods. For \(r\ge1\), $$ N_r(t) \;=\; \{\,u\in T \;:\; 1\le \delta(t,u)\le r\,\}, \qquad |N_1(t)|\le 6,\ |N_2(t)|\le 18,\ |N_3(t)|\le 36. $$ Only revealed tiles are in \(N_r\); \(t\) itself is not. GameMap.within, GameMap.neighbours (the latter uses the neighbour list the game reported).
River edges. Each tile has six edges, one per direction \(0..5\) = NE, E, SE, SW, W, NW. The game's flags say which side of a river the plot is on: IsWOfRiver (dump field river_w) means the plot is west of a river, so the river runs along its E edge; river_nw its SE edge; river_ne its SW edge. Its W, NW and NE edges are the E, SE, SW edges of the neighbour on that side. \(\varepsilon(t)\) is the number of river edges of \(t\). GameMap.river_edge(s). Checked: the edges agree with the game's IsRiver on every tile of both test maps, and every floodplain touches a river.
Rivers. Two tiles facing each other across a river edge are on the same river; consecutive edges of one river always share a tile, so the connected components of this relation are the rivers (rivers that meet are joined). \(\text{riv}(t)\) is the river of a tile touching one. GameMap.rivers.
Player and ownership. \(p\) is the local player. \(\text{own}(t)\) is the tile's owner (\(-1\): nobody). A tile is available to the player when \(\text{own}(t)\in\{-1,p\}\).
Reserved tiles. \(P\subseteq T\): tiles holding one of the player's own map tacks (the dump's PIN records; the planner's [MD] tacks are not included). Nothing is planned on them.

3. Yields and tile value

Yields. \(Y = \{\text{food}, \text{production}, \text{gold}, \text{science}, \text{culture}, \text{faith}\}\). \(y_{t,k}\ge0\) is the yield \(k\) of tile \(t\) as the game reports it (terrain, feature, visible resource, improvement).
Weights. \(w_k\ge0\), the value of one unit of yield \(k\) (objective.weights; default food 2, production 2, gold 0.5, science 2, culture 0.5, faith 0.5). For any yield vector \(\eta\), $$ v(\eta) = \sum_{k\in Y} w_k\,\eta_k, \qquad v(t) = v(y_{t,\cdot}). $$ Config.weighted.
Site value of a city on \(c\), with site radius \(\sigma\) (objective.site_radius, 2): $$ s_c \;=\; v(c) + \sum_{u\in N_\sigma(c)} v(u). $$ site_value. A proxy for what the city can work: the centre and the 18 tiles within 2.

4. Cities: existing and candidate

Known cities. \(K\subseteq T\): every revealed city centre (any player), i.e. tiles with is_city, plus the player's own cities. GameMap.city_positions.
Existing cities \(E\subseteq K\): the player's cities, from the dump's CITY records. For \(e\in E\), \(B_e\) is the set of district types it already has: the districts on tiles \(t\) with \(\text{own}(t)=p\) whose owning city (the tile's city field) is \(e\). existing_cities.
Open land. \(\Lambda\subseteq T\): tiles that are not water, not mountain, not impassable, not a natural wonder, hold no wonder, and are available to the player. Rules._open_land.
Candidate sites. With the minimum city distance \(\rho = \)CITY_MIN_RANGE \(=3\) from the game database, $$ C^0 = \{\, t\in\Lambda\setminus P \;:\; \text{feature}(t)\ \text{allows settling},\ \ \delta(t,k) > \rho\ \ \forall k\in K \,\}. $$ \(C\subseteq C^0\) keeps the \(M\) tiles with the largest \(s_c\) (problem.max_city_candidates, 400). Rules.can_found_city, prepare. "Allows settling": Features.Settlement \(\ne 0\).
Number of new cities. \(n\in\mathbb{N}\) (problem.n_cities); the default "max" means \(n = |C|\), so only the spacing rule limits how many are founded.

5. Districts and where they may go

District types. \(D\) = problem.districts: Campus, Industrial Zone, Commercial Hub, Holy Site, Theater, Encampment, Harbor, Aqueduct, Dam. (The Entertainment Complex is left out on purpose.) Subsets, read from the Districts table unless marked:
  • \(D^{\text{coast}}\): districts with Coast = 1 (the Harbor);
  • \(D^{\text{far}}\): districts with NoAdjacentCity = 1 (the Encampment);
  • \(D^{\text{aq}}\): districts with Aqueduct = 1 (the Aqueduct);
  • \(D^{\text{dam}}\): the Dam (engine rule: no flag in the database);
  • \(D^{\text{pop}}\): specialty districts, RequiresPopulation = 1: all planned districts but the Aqueduct and the Dam. For \(e\in E\), \(\beta_e = |B_e\cap D^{\text{pop}}|\) is the number it already has.
Fresh-water source. \(F\subseteq T\): tiles that touch a river, or have a lake, mountain or oasis neighbour. An engine rule for aqueducts, not in the database. Rules.fresh_water_source.
Valid tiles \(V_d\subseteq T\) for district \(d\): \(t\in V_d\) if and only if
  1. \(t\) holds no city and no district, and \(t\notin P\);
  2. \(t\) holds no luxury or strategic resource (an engine rule, not in the database);
  3. if \(d\in D^{\text{coast}}\): \(t\) is a coast (or lake) water tile, available to the player, not a wonder, with at least one land neighbour (AdjacentToLand); otherwise \(t\in\Lambda\);
  4. if \(d\in D^{\text{far}}\): no neighbour of \(t\) is a city centre, \(N_1(t)\cap K=\emptyset\);
  5. if \(d\) has District_RequiredFeatures rows, \(t\)'s feature is one of them (the Dam: floodplains, grassland or plains floodplains); likewise for District_ValidTerrains (none of the planned districts has any);
  6. if \(d\in D^{\text{dam}}\): \(\varepsilon(t)\ge 2\), a river along at least two edges (engine rule).
Rules.can_place_district.
District range. \(\rho_D\) = CITY_MAX_BUY_PLOT_RANGE \(=3\). For a city \(c\in C\cup E\) and a district \(d\), the tiles where \(c\)'s district \(d\) may go are $$ R_{d,c} \;=\; \begin{cases} \big(N_{\rho_D}(c)\cap V_d\big)\setminus N_1(c) & d\in D^{\text{far}},\\[2pt] N_1(c)\cap V_d\cap F & d\in D^{\text{aq}}\ \ \text{(next to its own city centre and fresh water)},\\[2pt] N_{\rho_D}(c)\cap V_d & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} $$ defined for every \(d\in D\) if \(c\in C\), and for \(d\in D\setminus B_c\) if \(c\in E\) (a city never gets a second district of a type it has). prepare (spots).

Improvements

Improvement types. \(I\) = problem.improvements: Farm, Mine, Quarry, Plantation, Camp, Pasture, Fishing Boats, Lumber Mill. Each \(i\) has, from the database (rows needing a technology or civic left out): its yield gain \(g_i\) (Improvement_YieldChanges: Farm +1 food, Mine +1 production, Quarry +1 production, Plantation +2 gold, Camp +2 gold, Pasture +1 production, Fishing Boats +1 food, Lumber Mill +2 production), its valid resources, features and terrains, and whether it goes on water (Domain). Rules._read_improvements.
Valid tiles \(W_i\subseteq T\): \(t\in W_i\) iff \(t\) holds no city, district or improvement, is not a mountain, impassable, wonder or natural wonder, is available to the player, \(t\notin P\), is water exactly when \(i\) is a sea improvement, and
  • if \(t\) has a (visible) resource: \(i\) is valid for it (Improvement_ValidResources), so a resource tile only takes its own improvement;
  • else if \(t\) has a feature: \(i\) allows that feature (Improvement_ValidFeatures: Farm on floodplains, Lumber Mill on forest);
  • else: \(i\) allows its terrain (Improvement_ValidTerrains: Farm on grassland or plains, Mine on hills).
Rules.can_place_improvement.
Improvement value. A city works only some tiles, and the ones it improves are the ones it works, so improving \(t\) with \(i\) is worth the whole tile: $$ h_{i,t} \;=\; v(g_i) \;+\; \mu\, v(t), $$ with \(\mu\) = objective.worked_tile_factor (1). Improving an already good tile beats improving a poor one. (\(v(t)\) is also in the city's site value \(s_c\); counting it again rewards working the best tiles.) improvement_value.
Candidates. For a city \(c\in C\cup E\), the options \(\{(i,t): t\in N_{\rho_D}(c)\cap W_i\}\) are ranked by \(h_{i,t}\) and only the best \(J\) are kept (problem.improvement_candidates, 6). \(G_{i,c}\) is the set of tiles kept for improvement \(i\). A simplification for speed: with all options, the full maps hit the time limit; with \(J=6\), one more than the five a city may build, a city can still swap a tile a district takes.

6. Adjacency

Each district type \(d\) has a set of adjacency rules \(\mathcal{A}_d\) read from the game database (District_Adjacencies joined with Adjacency_YieldChanges; Rules._read_adjacencies). Rules that need a technology or civic (or are made obsolete by one), Self rules and rules naming one specific resource are left out, so the rules kept are those that hold from the start of the game.

A rule \(\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_d\) is a tuple \((k_\alpha, \Delta_\alpha, m_\alpha, \pi_\alpha)\): yield type, change per step, tiles per step, and a predicate \(\pi_\alpha(u;\Pi)\) on a neighbouring tile \(u\), given a set \(\Pi\) of planned cities and districts (a map from tiles to district types, a city being DISTRICT_CITY_CENTER). Its count on tile \(t\) is $$ \text{cnt}_\alpha(t;\Pi) \;=\; \begin{cases} [\,t \text{ touches a river}\,] & \text{river rules,}\\ \big|\{\,u\in N_1(t) \;:\; \pi_\alpha(u;\Pi)\,\}\big| & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} $$ and the bonus of district \(d\) on \(t\) in yield \(k\) is $$ A_{d,k}(t;\Pi) \;=\; \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_d:\,k_\alpha=k} \Delta_\alpha \left\lfloor \frac{\text{cnt}_\alpha(t;\Pi)}{m_\alpha}\right\rfloor . $$ Rules.adjacency returns \(\big(A_{d,k}(t;\Pi)\big)_{k}\); these are the "+n" badges on tacks.

7. Decision variables

With the index set of district assignments $$ Z \;=\; \{\,(d,c,t) \;:\; c\in C,\ d\in D,\ t\in R_{d,c}\,\} \;\cup\; \{\,(d,e,t) \;:\; e\in E,\ d\in D\setminus B_e,\ t\in R_{d,e}\,\}, $$

$$ \begin{aligned} x_c &\in \{0,1\} && c \in C && \text{found a new city on } c && \texttt{city\_vars}\\ z_{d,c,t} &\in \{0,1\} && (d,c,t)\in Z && \text{city } c\text{'s district } d \text{ goes on } t && \texttt{district\_vars} \end{aligned} $$

Existing cities have no \(x\) variable: they are always there.

Improvements

With \(Q=\{(i,c,t): c\in C\cup E,\ t\in G_{i,c}\}\), and for district–improvement rules (§6, "\(+\Delta\) per \(m\) neighbouring \(i\)" for a planned district \(d\); with the defaults, the Industrial Zone's quarry, mine and lumber mill rules) the per-improvement value \(e_{d,i}=\sum w_k\Delta/m\) (the floor taken linearly: +0.5 per mine):

$$ \begin{aligned} \omega_{i,c,t} &\in \{0,1\} && (i,c,t)\in Q && \text{city } c \text{ improves } t \text{ with } i && \texttt{improvement\_vars}\\ q_{d,i,c,u} &\in [0,1] && (i,c,u)\in Q,\ N_1(u)\cap R_{d,c}\ne\emptyset && c\text{'s } i \text{ on } u \text{ touches } c\text{'s } d && \texttt{improvement\_bonus\_vars} \end{aligned} $$

Neighbour bonuses between planned districts of one city

Let \(\mathcal{B}\) be the set of pairs \((d,d')\in D\times D\), \(d\ne d'\), such that \(\mathcal{A}_d\) has a one-tile rule "\(+\Delta\) of yield \(k\) per neighbouring \(d'\)"; its weighted value is \(b_{d,d'}=\sum w_k\Delta\) over those rules (Rules.neighbour_bonuses). With the default districts: Industrial Zone next to Aqueduct or Dam (+2 production each, \(b=4\)) and Commercial Hub next to Harbor (+2 gold, \(b=1\)). For \((d,d')\in\mathcal{B}\), a city \(c\) and \(t\in R_{d,c}\) with \(N_1(t)\cap R_{d',c}\ne\emptyset\):

$$ \begin{aligned} y_{d,d',c,t} &\in [0,1] && \text{city } c\text{'s } d \text{ on } t \text{ gets the bonus from } c\text{'s } d' && \texttt{neighbour\_bonus\_vars} \end{aligned} $$

Continuous: with the links (6) and a positive objective coefficient, at an optimum \(y=\min\big(z_{d,c,t},\ \sum_{u} z_{d',c,u}\big)\in\{0,1\}\). Only the city's own \(d'\) is counted: one per city, so the count is 0 or 1 and the rule's floor is exact.

8. Objective

Maximise

$$ f(x,z) \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_{c\in C} s_c\,x_c}_{\texttt{site\_term}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{(d,c,t)\in Z} a_{d,c,t}\,z_{d,c,t}}_{\texttt{adjacency\_term}} \;-\; \underbrace{\lambda\!\!\sum_{(d,c,t)\in Z}\!\! v(t)\,z_{d,c,t}}_{\texttt{covered\_tile\_term}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum b_{d,d'}\,y_{d,d',c,t}}_{\texttt{neighbour\_bonus\_term}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{Q} h_{i,t}\,\omega_{i,c,t}}_{\texttt{improvement\_term}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum e_{d,i}\,q_{d,i,c,u}}_{\texttt{improvement\_bonus\_term}} \;+\; \underbrace{\beta_C\sum_{c\in C} x_c}_{\texttt{city\_bonus\_term}} \;+\; \underbrace{\beta_D\!\!\sum_{(d,c,t)\in Z}\!\! z_{d,c,t}}_{\texttt{district\_bonus\_term}} $$
  • \(\lambda\) (objective.loss_factor, 0.5): the share of a covered tile's yields counted as lost. A district replaces the tile's own yields; the city still gets some value back through its buildings, hence \(\lambda<1\).
  • \(\beta_C\), \(\beta_D\) (objective.city_bonus 100, objective.district_bonus 10): placement bonuses. Without them a district whose \(a_{d,c,t}-\lambda v(t)\) is negative would not be built (the constraints only say "at most"); with them, building is preferred. See §11.

9. Constraints

(1)Number of new cities · max_cities

$$ \sum_{c\in C} x_c \;\le\; n . $$

(2)City spacing · city_spacing (only when \(n\ge2\))

Two new cities must be more than \(\rho\) apart. Pairwise, this is

$$ x_a + x_b \;\le\; 1 \qquad \forall\, a,b\in C,\ a\ne b,\ \delta(a,b)\le\rho. \tag{2'} $$

The model writes the same condition with clique inequalities, whose linear relaxation is much tighter. Let \(\mathcal{T}\) be the triangles of \(T\): sets \(\tau=\{t_1,t_2,t_3\}\) of three mutually adjacent revealed tiles. For \(\tau\in\mathcal{T}\),

$$ Q_\tau \;=\; C\cap\Big(\tau\ \cup\ N_1(t_1)\cup N_1(t_2)\cup N_1(t_3)\Big), \qquad |Q_\tau|\le 12, $$

and \(\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_\tau : |Q_\tau|\ge2\}\). The constraints are

$$ \sum_{c\in Q} x_c \;\le\; 1 \quad \forall\, Q\in\mathcal{Q}, \qquad\qquad x_a + x_b \;\le\; 1 \quad \forall\, \{a,b\}\in U, $$

where \(U\) is the set of conflicting pairs of (2') not contained in any \(Q\in\mathcal{Q}\).

Claim 1. Every \(Q_\tau\) is a clique of the conflict graph: \(\delta(a,b)\le 3=\rho\) for all \(a,b\in Q_\tau\).
Proof. \(a\) is within 1 of some \(t_i\), \(b\) within 1 of some \(t_j\), and \(\delta(t_i,t_j)\le1\); by the triangle inequality \(\delta(a,b)\le 1+1+1=3\). (The code also checks it.) \(\square\)

Claim 2. If \(\delta(a,b)\le 3\) and some shortest path from \(a\) to \(b\), together with the third vertex of a triangle on it, is revealed, then \(\{a,b\}\subseteq Q_\tau\) for some \(\tau\).
Proof. Let \(a=u_0,u_1,\dots,u_\ell=b\) be the path, \(\ell\le3\). If \(\ell=3\), take a triangle containing the edge \(u_1u_2\): \(a\in N_1(u_1)\), \(b\in N_1(u_2)\). If \(\ell=2\), take a triangle containing \(u_1\). If \(\ell=1\), take a triangle containing the edge \(ab\). On the hex grid every edge lies in two triangles. \(\square\)

Consequence. The clique rows together with the pairwise rows for \(U\) have exactly the same integer solutions as (2'). \(U\) is empty inside the revealed area; it only matters at its edge, around unrevealed tiles.

Why it matters. Under (2') the point \(x_c=\tfrac12\) for all \(c\) is feasible for the relaxation, so its bound counts about half of all candidates as cities; one clique row over 12 candidates cuts their total to 1. With the pairwise form HiGHS spent minutes proving the packing on the full maps; with cliques they solved in 10–15 s (20–30 s since the neighbour bonuses of §7 were added).

Spacing from existing cities (any player) is enforced through \(C\subseteq C^0\).

(3)One district of each type per city, only if the city exists · district_needs_city

$$ \sum_{t\in R_{d,c}} z_{d,c,t} \;\le\; x_c \quad \forall\, c\in C,\ d\in D, \qquad\qquad \sum_{t\in R_{d,e}} z_{d,e,t} \;\le\; 1 \quad \forall\, e\in E,\ d\in D\setminus B_e . $$

(4)At most one thing per tile · one_thing_per_tile

$$ [t\in C]\,x_t \;+\; \sum_{(d,c):\,(d,c,t)\in Z} z_{d,c,t} \;\le\; 1 \qquad \forall\, t\in T $$

Written only for tiles with at least two terms. It stops two districts (of one city or of different cities) from sharing a tile, and a district from going on a tile chosen as a new city centre. Existing city centres and districts are already outside every \(V_d\).

(5)No Encampment next to another planned city · not_next_to_planned_city

$$ z_{d,c,t} + x_{c'} \;\le\; 1 \qquad \forall\, (d,c,t)\in Z,\ d\in D^{\text{far}},\ c'\in C\cap N_1(t),\ c'\ne c . $$

Its own city is already excluded through \(R_{d,c}\), and existing cities through \(V_d\).

(6)Neighbour bonus only when both districts are there · neighbour_bonus_link

$$ y_{d,d',c,t} \;\le\; z_{d,c,t}, \qquad y_{d,d',c,t} \;\le\; \sum_{u\in N_1(t)\cap R_{d',c}} z_{d',c,u}, \qquad \sum_{t} y_{d,d',c,t} \;\le\; \sum_{u\in R_{d',c}} z_{d',c,u} . $$

The third row is implied for integer \(z\) (one \(d'\) per city can neighbour one \(d\)) but tightens the relaxation: it halved the full maps' solve time (47 → 22 s on continents) without changing the optimal objective.

(7)One Dam per river · one_dam_per_river

$$ \sum_{(d,c,t)\in Z:\ d\in D^{\text{dam}},\ \text{riv}(t)=r} z_{d,c,t} \;\le\; \begin{cases} 0 & \text{a Dam already stands on river } r,\\ 1 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases} \qquad \forall\ \text{rivers } r . $$

Across all cities, new and existing. Rivers that meet count as one, which may forbid a Dam the game would allow on a tributary.

(8)Population limit · specialty_limit

$$ \sum_{d\in D^{\text{pop}}}\ \sum_{t\in R_{d,c}} z_{d,c,t} \;\le\; K\,x_c \quad \forall c\in C, \qquad\qquad \sum_{d\in D^{\text{pop}}}\ \sum_{t\in R_{d,e}} z_{d,e,t} \;\le\; \max(0,\,K-\beta_e) \quad \forall e\in E . $$

The game allows a city \(1+\lfloor(\text{population}-1)/3\rfloor\) specialty districts (DISTRICT_POPULATION_REQUIRED_PER = 3). The model uses a fixed \(K\) (problem.max_specialty_districts, 3, i.e. a city of 7–9 citizens), so each city keeps its best \(K\); the Aqueduct and Dam don't count.

(9)Improvements per city · improvement_limit

$$ \sum_{(i,t)} \omega_{i,c,t} \;\le\; L\,x_c,\quad \sum_i \omega_{i,c,t} \;\le\; x_c\ \ \forall t \qquad (c\in C), \qquad\qquad \sum_{(i,t)} \omega_{i,e,t} \;\le\; \max(0,\,L-\ell_e) \qquad (e\in E), $$

\(L\) = problem.max_improvements (5); \(\ell_e\) = improvements already on the tiles city \(e\) owns. The per-tile row is implied but tightens the relaxation. Constraint (4) also covers improvements: one city, district or improvement per tile.

(10)Improvement bonus only when both are there · improvement_bonus_link

$$ q_{d,i,c,u} \;\le\; \omega_{i,c,u}, \qquad q_{d,i,c,u} \;\le\; \sum_{t\in N_1(u)\cap R_{d,c}} z_{d,c,t} . $$

One \(d\) per city, so the right-hand sums are at most 1 and no big-M is needed; \(\sum_u q\) counts the improvements next to the city's \(d\). (A first form with one variable per district tile and a bound of 6 left the full maps 12% from optimal at the time limit.)

10. The full programme

$$ \begin{aligned} \max_{x,\,z,\,y,\,\omega,\,q}\quad & \sum_{c\in C}(s_c+\beta_C)\,x_c \;+\; \sum_{(d,c,t)\in Z}\big(a_{d,c,t}-\lambda\,v(t)+\beta_D\big)\,z_{d,c,t} \;+\; \sum b_{d,d'}\,y_{d,d',c,t} \;+\; \sum_Q h_{i,t}\,\omega_{i,c,t} \;+\; \sum e_{d,i}\,q_{d,i,c,u}\\[4pt] \text{s.t.}\quad & \textstyle\sum_{c\in C} x_c \le n && (1)\\ & \textstyle\sum_{c\in Q} x_c \le 1 \ \ \forall Q\in\mathcal{Q}, \qquad x_a+x_b\le 1 \ \ \forall\{a,b\}\in U && (2)\\ & \textstyle\sum_{t\in R_{d,c}} z_{d,c,t} \le x_c \ \ \forall c\in C,\, d\in D && (3a)\\ & \textstyle\sum_{t\in R_{d,e}} z_{d,e,t} \le 1 \ \ \forall e\in E,\, d\in D\setminus B_e && (3b)\\ & \textstyle [t\in C]\,x_t + \sum_{(d,c):(d,c,t)\in Z} z_{d,c,t} \le 1 \ \ \forall t\in T && (4)\\ & z_{d,c,t} + x_{c'} \le 1 \ \ \forall (d,c,t)\in Z,\ d\in D^{\text{far}},\ c'\in C\cap N_1(t)\setminus\{c\} && (5)\\ & y_{d,d',c,t} \le z_{d,c,t},\ \ y_{d,d',c,t} \le \textstyle\sum_{u\in N_1(t)\cap R_{d',c}} z_{d',c,u},\ \ \sum_t y_{d,d',c,t} \le \sum_{u} z_{d',c,u} && (6)\\ & \textstyle\sum_{d\in D^{\text{dam}},\,\text{riv}(t)=r} z_{d,c,t} \le 1 \ (0 \text{ if dammed}) \ \ \forall \text{ rivers } r && (7)\\ & \textstyle\sum_{d\in D^{\text{pop}},\,t} z_{d,c,t} \le K x_c \ \forall c\in C, \qquad \sum_{d\in D^{\text{pop}},\,t} z_{d,e,t} \le \max(0,K-\beta_e) \ \forall e\in E && (8)\\ & \textstyle\sum_{(i,t)} \omega_{i,c,t} \le L x_c,\ \sum_i \omega_{i,c,t}\le x_c \ \forall c\in C, \qquad \sum_{(i,t)} \omega_{i,e,t} \le \max(0,L-\ell_e) \ \forall e\in E && (9)\\ & q_{d,i,c,u} \le \omega_{i,c,u},\ \ q_{d,i,c,u} \le \textstyle\sum_{t\in N_1(u)\cap R_{d,c}} z_{d,c,t} && (10)\\ & x\in\{0,1\}^{C},\quad z\in\{0,1\}^{Z},\quad \omega\in\{0,1\}^{Q},\quad y, q\in[0,1] \end{aligned} $$

11. Properties

  • Always feasible. \(x=0, z=0\) satisfies every constraint, so the solver always returns a plan (empty when no tile qualifies).
  • Districts are built whenever a tile is free. Adding one district to a feasible solution, on a tile \(t\in R_{d,c}\) that holds nothing planned and that (5) and (7) allow, for a founded city that lacks \(d\) and is under its population limit (8), changes \(f\) by \(a_{d,c,t}-\lambda v(t)+\beta_D \ge \beta_D-\lambda v(t)\), since \(a\ge0\). This is positive whenever \(v(t)<\beta_D/\lambda=20\), true of every tile in practice. So at an optimum a city lacks a district only if every tile of \(R_{d,c}\) is taken, or (for a specialty district) it already has \(K\) of them.
  • Cities are a soft priority. \(\beta_C=100\) is not a proven bound: founding one more city can displace other cities and districts, and the model accepts it only if the loss elsewhere is below \(\beta_C+s_c\). On the test maps it packs as many cities as the spacing allows.
  • Determinism. For a given map and settings the programme is fixed and HiGHS's result is reproducible; among equally good plans its choice is arbitrary but repeatable.

12. Score and evaluation

The score reported for a plan leaves the bonuses out and is recomputed in plain Python by evaluate, for any plan (the MIP's or the greedy stub's):

$$ S \;=\; \sum_{c\ \text{new}} s_c \;+\; \sum_{(d,c,t)\ \text{planned}} v\big(A_{d,\cdot}(t;\Pi^{\text{all}})\big) \;-\; \lambda\!\!\sum_{(d,c,t)\ \text{planned}}\!\! v(t) \;+\; \sum_{(i,c,t)\ \text{planned}} h_{i,t}, $$

where \(\Pi^{\text{all}}\) holds every planned city centre, district and improvement. (Mines and lumber mills count with the game's floor here, \(\lfloor\text{count}/2\rfloor\), against \(0.5\) each in the MIP, so \(S\) can be slightly below the MIP's value for an odd count.) Every predicate is monotone in \(\Pi\), so \(A(t;\Pi^{\text{all}})\ge A(t;\{c\mapsto\text{city centre}\})\) and

$$ S \;\ge\; f(x^\star,z^\star,y^\star) - \beta_C\,|x^\star| - \beta_D\,|z^\star| , $$

(with the neighbour bonuses \(b\,y\) included in \(f\)), with equality when the only planned-next-to-planned bonuses are those of §7, the only exception to \(\ge\) being the mine and lumber-mill rounding above. The tests check the equality on maps where that holds. The "+n" badges on tacks are the per-yield bonuses \(A_{d,k}(t;\Pi^{\text{all}})\).

15. Timing (optional) · [timing]

With timing.periods \(H>0\) (config/timing.toml sets 6) the plan also says when each city and district is ready. The objective is the static objective \(f\) of §8 minus the value lost by being late, written so that one period (\(H=1\)) is exactly the static model (Proposition 2). Code names are given with each symbol.

Time

Horizon \(\Theta\) turns (horizon_turns, 120), cut into \(H\) periods \(\mathcal H=\{1,\dots,H\}\) of \(\theta=\Theta/H\) turns (turns_per_period). Period \(p\) covers turns \((p-1)\theta+1\) to \(p\theta\).
Border delay of a tile at distance \(r\) from its city, in whole periods, rounded down: \(\nu_r=\lfloor\theta_r/\theta\rfloor\), with \(\theta_r\) = ring_delay_turns\([r]\) = 0, 0, 15, 35 for \(r=0,\dots,3\). (\(\theta=20\): \(\nu=0,0,0,1\).) Timing.delay
Settler schedule \(\bar n_p\): the most new cities founded by the end of period \(p\), simulated turn by turn so it doesn't depend on \(H\). With \(n_0=n_{\text{start}}\) (settlers_at_start, 1), \(g_0=0\) and \(\theta_S\) = turns_per_settler (40), for \(\tau=1,\dots,\Theta\): $$ g_\tau = g_{\tau-1}-\lfloor g_{\tau-1}\rfloor+\frac{|E|+n_{\tau-1}}{\theta_S},\qquad n_\tau=n_{\tau-1}+\lfloor g_\tau\rfloor,\qquad \bar n_p = n_{\lceil p\theta\rceil}. $$ (The leftover \(g-\lfloor g\rfloor\) carries over.) With \(E=\emptyset\): 11 new cities by turn 120. settler_schedule
Build rate \(\theta/\theta_D\) districts per city per period, \(\theta_D\) = turns_per_district (20).

Variables · timing_vars

The static variables \(x, z, \omega, y, q\) keep their meaning ("where"). "When" is added per city and per (district, city), with no tile index:

Shorthand: \(\Phi_{c,p}=\sum_{q=1}^{p}\phi_{c,q}\) (founded by \(p\); \(\Phi_{c,p}=0\) for \(p\le 0\)) for \(c\in C\), and \(\Phi_{e,p}=1\) for \(e\in E\), \(p\ge0\) (the player's cities exist). The district's static value on the tile \(z\) picked, with its bonus, and an upper bound on it:

$$ A_{d,c} \;=\; \sum_{t\in R_{d,c}}\big(\beta_D+a_{d,c,t}-\lambda v(t)\big)\,z_{d,c,t} \;+\; \sum_{d',t} b_{d,d'}\,y_{d,d',c,t} \;+\; \sum_{i,u} e_{d,i}\,q_{d,i,c,u}, $$ $$ \bar A_{d,c} \;=\; \max_{t\in R_{d,c}}\big(\beta_D+a_{d,c,t}-\lambda v(t)\big) \;+\; \sum_{d'} b_{d,d'} \;+\; L_c\sum_{i} e_{d,i}, $$

sums over the \(y\), \(q\) variables that exist for \((d,c)\); \(L_c\) = the improvements city \(c\) may still get (\(L\), or \(L-\ell_e\)). By (3) at most one \(z_{d,c,\cdot}\) is 1, and (6), (10) bound the bonus terms, so \(A_{d,c}\le\bar A_{d,c}\). _district_value

Constraints

All for \(p\in\mathcal H\) (with \(\psi_{d,c,0}=0\)).

(T1)Links · timing_link

$$ \sum_{p\in\mathcal H}\phi_{c,p}\le x_c\quad(c\in C),\qquad \psi_{d,c,p-1}\le\psi_{d,c,p},\qquad \psi_{d,c,H}\le\sum_{t\in R_{d,c}} z_{d,c,t}. $$

A city has at most one period, a district is ready only if placed and stays ready. \(\le\) rather than \(=\): a placement with no period comes after the horizon (tacks: later). Then every placement has a schedule (all after the horizon), which HiGHS's heuristics need: with \(=\) they found almost no plans.

(T2)Settlers · settler_capacity

$$ \sum_{c\in C}\Phi_{c,p}\;\le\;\bar n_p . $$

(T3)District with or after its city · district_after_city

$$ \psi_{d,c,p}\;\le\;\Phi_{c,p}\qquad(c\in C). $$

(T4)Build rate · build_capacity

$$ \sum_{d}\psi_{d,c,p}\;\le\;\frac{\theta}{\theta_D}\sum_{q=1}^{p}(p-q+1)\,\phi_{c,q}\quad(c\in C),\qquad \sum_{d}\psi_{d,e,p}\;\le\;\frac{\theta}{\theta_D}\,p\quad(e\in E). $$

A city founded in period \(q\) has \(p-q+1\) periods by the end of \(p\) (its founding period included), so at most \(\theta/\theta_D\) districts per period since then.

(T5)Borders · ring_delay

$$ \psi_{d,c,p}+\sum_{t\in R_{d,c}:\ \delta(c,t)=r} z_{d,c,t}\;-\;\Phi_{c,\,p-\nu_r}\;\le\;1 \qquad(c\in C,\ r\ \text{with}\ \nu_r\ge1). $$

If the district is ready by \(p\) and sits at distance \(r\), the city was founded by \(p-\nu_r\). Improvements get the same delay through \(\gamma\) (T6).

(T6)Lateness · lateness_link

$$ \chi_{d,c,p}\;\ge\;A_{d,c}-\bar A_{d,c}\,\psi_{d,c,p},\qquad \gamma_{i,c,t,p}\;\le\;\omega_{i,c,t},\qquad \gamma_{i,c,t,p}\;\le\;\Phi_{c,\,p-\nu_{\delta(c,t)}} . $$

Objective · lateness_term

$$ f_H \;=\; f\;-\;\frac1H\sum_{p\in\mathcal H}\Big[\sum_{c\in C}s_c\,(x_c-\Phi_{c,p}) \;+\;\sum_{(d,c)}\chi_{d,c,p} \;+\;\sum_{(i,c,t)\in Q,\ c\in C} h_{i,t}\,\big(\omega_{i,c,t}-\gamma_{i,c,t,p}\big)\Big] $$

\(f\) is the static objective of §8, unchanged (all its terms and bonuses). \(\gamma_{i,c,t,p}\) is 0 where it doesn't exist (\(p\le\nu_r\)). Each bracket is \(\ge0\) at any feasible point (by T1, T6), so \(f_H\le f\). The player's existing cities and their improvements are there from the start: no lateness.

Proposition 1 (what lateness costs). At an optimum with \(x, z, \omega, \phi, \psi\) integer, write \(p_c\) for the period of city \(c\) and \(p_{d,c}=\min\{p:\psi_{d,c,p}=1\}\) for its district (\(H+1\) if none: after the horizon). Then, of \(f\), the plan loses $$ \frac{p_c-1}{H}\,s_c \ \text{per city},\qquad \frac{p_{d,c}-1}{H}\max(0,A_{d,c}) \ \text{per district},\qquad \frac{\min\big(H,\;p_c+\nu_{\delta(c,t)}-1\big)}{H}\,h_{i,t} \ \text{per improvement of a new city}. $$ Proof. City: \(x_c-\Phi_{c,p}=1\) exactly for \(p So something ready in period \(p\) loses \((p-1)/H\) of its value, \(1/H\) for each period it isn't there, and after the horizon all of it. In the district's \(A\) the bonus \(\beta_D\) is counted too, so every district, an Aqueduct as well, is worth being early. evaluate() uses these formulas: its late is the yields part, its late_bonus the \(\beta_D\) part (left out of the score, like the bonuses), so MIP objective = score + bonuses − late_bonus (tested).

Proposition 2 (one period is the static model). Let \(H=1\), and suppose \(\Theta/\theta_D\ge K+|D\setminus D^{\text{pop}}|\) (6 ≥ 3 + 2 by default) and \(\theta_r<\Theta\) for all \(r\). Then the timed model is the static model with the one extra constraint \(\sum_c x_c\le\bar n_1\); in particular, if \(\bar n_1\) doesn't bind, the optimal values are equal and every static optimum (with its schedule) is a timed optimum.
Proof. With \(H=1\), \(\theta=\Theta\) and \(\nu_r=\lfloor\theta_r/\Theta\rfloor=0\), so (T5) is empty and \(\gamma_{i,c,t,1}\) exists for every option. Take any point \((x,z,\omega,y,q)\) feasible for the static model with \(\sum x_c\le\bar n_1\), and set \(\phi_{c,1}=x_c\), \(\psi_{d,c,1}=\sum_t z_{d,c,t}\), \(\gamma=\omega\), \(\chi=0\). (T1), (T2) hold; (T3): \(\sum_t z_{d,c,t}\le x_c\) is (3); (T4): a city has at most \(K\) specialty districts (8) and one of each other type (3), so \(\sum_d\psi_{d,c,1}\le K+|D\setminus D^{\text{pop}}|\le(\Theta/\theta_D)\,x_c\); (T6): \(A\le\bar A\,\psi\), since \(\psi=\sum_t z_{d,c,t}\in\{0,1\}\) and either both sides are 0 or \(A\le\bar A\); \(\gamma\le\Phi_{c,1}=x_c\) by (9). Every bracket of \(f_1\) is then 0, so \(f_1=f\). Conversely \(f_1\le f\) at every feasible point, and the timed constraints include the static ones. \(\square\)
(The step \(A\le\bar A\,\psi\) uses integrality for the \(q\) terms: \(\sum_u q_{d,i,c,u}\le L_c\) holds only once the district is placed. At a fractional \(z\) in the LP relaxation \(\chi\) can be positive, so the two relaxations can differ slightly.)
(Test test_one_period_is_the_static_model: equal objectives.) With more periods, timing moves the placements: a tile that can be ready sooner loses less.

The timed programme

$$ \begin{aligned} \max\quad & f(x,z,y,\omega,q)\;-\;\frac1H\sum_{p\in\mathcal H}\Big[\sum_{c\in C}s_c(x_c-\Phi_{c,p})+\sum_{(d,c)}\chi_{d,c,p} +\sum_{(i,c,t)\in Q,\,c\in C}h_{i,t}(\omega_{i,c,t}-\gamma_{i,c,t,p})\Big]\\[4pt] \text{s.t.}\quad & \text{(1)–(10) of \S 10}\\ & \textstyle\sum_p\phi_{c,p}\le x_c,\quad \psi_{d,c,p-1}\le\psi_{d,c,p},\quad \psi_{d,c,H}\le\sum_t z_{d,c,t} && \text{(T1)}\\ & \textstyle\sum_{c\in C}\Phi_{c,p}\le\bar n_p && \text{(T2)}\\ & \psi_{d,c,p}\le\Phi_{c,p}\quad(c\in C) && \text{(T3)}\\ & \textstyle\sum_d\psi_{d,c,p}\le\frac{\theta}{\theta_D}\sum_{q\le p}(p-q+1)\phi_{c,q}\ (c\in C),\quad \sum_d\psi_{d,e,p}\le\frac{\theta}{\theta_D}p\ (e\in E) && \text{(T4)}\\ & \textstyle\psi_{d,c,p}+\sum_{t:\delta(c,t)=r}z_{d,c,t}-\Phi_{c,p-\nu_r}\le1\quad(c\in C,\ \nu_r\ge1) && \text{(T5)}\\ & \chi_{d,c,p}\ge A_{d,c}-\bar A_{d,c}\psi_{d,c,p},\quad \gamma_{i,c,t,p}\le\omega_{i,c,t},\quad \gamma_{i,c,t,p}\le\Phi_{c,p-\nu_{\delta(c,t)}} && \text{(T6)}\\ & \phi,\psi\ \text{binary},\quad 0\le\chi_{d,c,p}\le\bar A_{d,c},\quad 0\le\gamma\le1 \end{aligned} $$

Size: \(H|C|\) of \(\phi\), \(H\) per (district, city) of \(\psi\) and \(\chi\), at most \(H\) per new city's improvement option of \(\gamma\). The only big-M is \(\bar A\) in (T6), and it sits on a penalty: rows (1)–(10) are unchanged, and lateness only corrects the objective.

Solving · timing.mode

Three solves, each logged as a stage: where (the static model); when (the timed model with \(x, z, \omega\) fixed to that plan: a small scheduling problem, 0.1–0.3 s); and, in joint mode (the default), the timed model with everything free, starting from that plan (through highspy; scipy.optimize.milp can't pass a start). The joint solve can only improve on the start. sequential stops after the second. The log says what each stage gave: static plan: … and static plan, scheduled: … (counts, and how many are within the horizon), starting from the plan above and HiGHS's MIP start solution is feasible, then joint: +5.1% over the static plan, scheduled (830.46 -> 872.67) or joint: kept its start. Beyond 8 periods it warns: the joint model grows with \(H\) and at 24 periods HiGHS doesn't finish its first LP in 60 s, so the joint stage returns its start (still a valid, scheduled plan). With 12 periods, continents-revealed gains 5.1%; continents-radius6 keeps its start.

Without a start, the joint model found no plan at all in 60 s on the radius-18 and full maps. 16. Not modelled

  • Adjacency between planned districts, beyond a district and its own city centre and the one-tile neighbour bonuses of §7 within a city (Industrial Zone–Aqueduct, Commercial Hub–Harbor): the "+1 per 2 districts" rules, and districts of different planned cities. Making \(a\) depend on other \(z\) in general needs coupling variables, e.g. integers \(\kappa_{\alpha,t}\) with \(m_\alpha\kappa_{\alpha,t}\le\) (matching existing neighbours) \(+\sum_{u\in N_1(t)}\sum z_{\cdot,\cdot,u}\) and \(\kappa_{\alpha,t}\le\lfloor 6/m_\alpha\rfloor\sum_c z_{d,c,t}\). A first try weakened the relaxation and slowed HiGHS, so it waits. \(S\) (§12) already counts these bonuses.
  • Tile ownership and timing (measured after each solve by validate.py: 10–19% of districts and improvements on the test maps sit next to another planned city's centre, a tile that city claims when founded, unless it is already owned: a tile never moves between a player's cities, so such a placement holds if its city owns the tile, through borders or a bought tile, before the neighbour is founded; founding order decides). In the game a district goes on a tile its city owns; borders grow over time or through bought tiles, and a tile belongs to one city. The model allows any free tile within 3 and lets a district sit in another planned city's range.
  • Improvements: technology- and civic-gated rules (Farms on hills, bonus yields later in the game), Farm adjacency, removing a feature to improve a tile, and builder charges are not modelled; only each city's best \(J\) options are considered; a district-improvement bonus counts only the city's own improvements.
  • Population over time. Every city gets the same \(K\) specialty districts; in the game the limit grows with population, and which city grows how big (food, housing) is not modelled.
  • Timing (§15, optional): coarse periods; fixed rates (no production, gold purchases, chops); a settler costs nothing to its city; population growth only through the fixed limit \(K\); a district's whole value (its covered tile's loss too) counts from when it is ready; builders and technologies don't limit improvements; the joint solve often can't improve its start within 60 s on maps beyond the revealed ones.
  • Site value is a proxy. No housing (fresh water, coast), amenities, growth over time or future improvements; sites of cities 4 apart can count the same tile twice.
  • Aqueduct and Dam housing: their own value (housing, and the Dam's flood protection) is not counted; they are placed for the Industrial Zone bonus and the district bonus. The Aqueduct is also planned for cities already on fresh water.
  • Rivers that meet count as one river for the one-Dam rule.
  • Other districts: Entertainment Complex (left out on purpose), Canal, Government Plaza, Diplomatic Quarter, Spaceport, Aerodrome, Neighborhood, Preserve, Water Park; civilisation-unique districts; wonders; improvements.
  • Technology- or civic-gated adjacency, and civilisation or leader bonuses.
  • Foreign districts count in adjacency like the player's; the game's own rule is not checked yet.
  • Fog: only revealed tiles exist, so hidden cities or borders can be planned over.
  • Distance and cost: new cities can be anywhere in the revealed area; no travel time or settler cost.
  • Candidate cap: on the full maps only the 400 best sites are candidates, which caps the number of cities.
