Placement model

The MIP the planner solves today (src/civ6_planner/model.py), solved with HiGHS through scipy.optimize.milp. Deliberately simple: as many cities as fit, each with up to one of seven districts (Campus, Industrial Zone, Commercial Hub, Holy Site, Theater, Encampment, Harbor), optimal in 0.4–15 s on the test maps.

Derived coefficients

Weighted value of a yield vector, with weights \(w_k\) (placeholders, see Parameters):

$$ v(t) \;=\; \sum_{k\in Y} w_k\, y_{t,k} $$

Site value of a city on \(c\): its centre plus every revealed tile within 2.

$$ s_c \;=\; v(c) + \sum_{t \in N_2(c)} v(t) $$

Adjacency of district \(d\) on tile \(t\) for the city on \(c\), from the game database (District_Adjacencies, see the table below). For each rule \(\rho\) of \(d\) giving \(\delta_\rho\) of yield \(k_\rho\) per \(m_\rho\) matching neighbours, with \(\text{cnt}_\rho(t; c)\) the matching neighbours of \(t\):

$$ a_{d,c,t} \;=\; \sum_{\rho} w_{k_\rho}\,\delta_\rho \left\lfloor \frac{\text{cnt}_\rho(t; c)}{m_\rho} \right\rfloor $$

Neighbours count as districts when they already hold a city or a district, or when they are \(c\) itself (the planned city centre). That is why \(a\) depends on \(c\). The floor is exact because \(a_{d,c,t}\) is a constant computed before solving.

Objective coefficient of placing district \(d\) of city \(c\) on \(t\):

$$ g_{d,c,t} \;=\; a_{d,c,t} \;-\; \lambda\, v(t) \;+\; \beta_D $$

Decision variables

In model.py: \(x\) is city_vars, \(z\) is district_vars. The model is built from three lists, VARIABLES, CONSTRAINTS and OBJECTIVE, with one function per variable family, constraint and objective term; comment out a line to drop that constraint or term.

$$ \begin{aligned} x_c &\in \{0,1\} && c \in C && \text{found a city on } c\\ z_{d,c,t} &\in \{0,1\} && d\in D,\; c\in C,\; t \in R_{d,c} && \text{district } d \text{ of city } c \text{ goes on } t \end{aligned} $$

Objective

$$ \max \;\; \sum_{c\in C} \big(s_c + \beta_C\big)\, x_c \;+\; \sum_{d\in D}\sum_{c\in C}\sum_{t\in R_{d,c}} g_{d,c,t}\; z_{d,c,t} $$

Terms, in OBJECTIVE: \(s_c x_c\) site_term, \(a_{d,c,t} z\) adjacency_term, \(-\lambda v(t) z\) covered_tile_term, \(\beta_C x\) city_bonus_term, \(\beta_D z\) district_bonus_term.

The reported score leaves the bonuses out: site value + adjacency − \(\lambda\,v\)(covered tiles). \(\beta_C\) and \(\beta_D\) are large enough that cities and districts are always placed when they can be.

Constraints

(1)Number of cities · max_cities

$$ \sum_{c\in C} x_c \;\le\; n $$

(2)City spacing (only added when \(n>1\)) · city_spacing

$$ \sum_{c\in K} x_c \;\le\; 1 \qquad \forall\, K\in\mathcal{K} $$

Every pair of candidates within 3 of each other lies in some clique \(K\), so this says the same as \(x_a + x_b \le 1\) for each such pair, with a much tighter relaxation. With many cities the pairwise form left HiGHS proving the packing for minutes; the clique form solves the full maps in 10–15 s. Pairs no clique covers (around unrevealed tiles) keep a pairwise row.

(3)At most one district of each type per city, and only if the city is founded · district_needs_city

$$ \sum_{t\in R_{d,c}} z_{d,c,t} \;\le\; x_c \quad \forall\, d\in D,\ c\in C \qquad\qquad \sum_{t\in R_{d,e}} z_{d,e,t} \;\le\; 1 \quad \forall\, e\in E,\ d\in D_e $$

Variables \(z_{d,e,t}\) for existing cities are created like those of new cities (same \(R_{d,e}\), same objective coefficient); their centre is already on the map, so it counts in adjacency without being planned.

(4)One thing per tile · one_thing_per_tile

$$ x_t\,[t\in C] \;+\; \sum_{d\in D}\ \sum_{c\,:\,t\in R_{d,c}} z_{d,c,t} \;\le\; 1 \qquad \forall\, t\in T $$

(5)No Encampment next to another planned city · not_next_to_planned_city

$$ z_{d,c,t} + x_{c'} \;\le\; 1 \qquad \forall\, d\in D^{\text{far}},\ t\in R_{d,c},\ c'\in N_1(t)\cap C,\ c'\ne c $$

Placement rules (terrain, resources, ownership, existing cities) are applied by not creating the variable at all, through \(C\) and \(V\).

Adjacency rules used

Read from DebugGameplay.sqlite at run time (Rules._read_adjacencies) for every planned district. Kept: neighbouring terrain, feature, district (any, or a given type), improvement, resource class, sea resource, wonder, natural wonder, and a river on the district's own tile. Left out: rules needing a tech or civic (or made obsolete by one), and Self rules. Main rules per district:

DistrictAdjacency
Campus+1 science per mountain, +1 per 2 rainforest, +2 per reef / geothermal fissure, +1 per 2 districts

Commercial Hub+2 gold on a river, +2 next to a Harbor, +1 per 2 districts

Not modelled yet

  • Adjacency between planned districts, other than a district and its own city centre. Exact for one city with one district; with more it needs integer variables \(k_{d,t}\) with \(m\,k_{d,t} \le\) (existing + planned districts next to \(t\)) and \(k_{d,t}\le \lfloor 6/m\rfloor \sum_c z_{d,c,t}\). A first try made the relaxation weak and HiGHS slow, so it waits. evaluate already counts these bonuses when scoring a plan.
  • The site value is a proxy: no housing (fresh water, coast), no amenities, no growth over time, no tile improvements.
  • Other districts (Entertainment Complex left out on purpose; Aqueduct, Dam, Canal, Government Plaza, Diplomatic Quarter, Spaceport, Aerodrome, Neighborhood, Preserve, Water Park), unique districts, wonders, improvements.
  • Population: the game allows one district per 3 citizens (plus one); the plan gives every city all of them.
  • Ownership: a district must go on a tile the city owns, which comes with border growth or buying tiles.
  • On the full maps only the 400 best city sites are candidates, which caps the number of cities (56 there).
  • Site values of cities 4 apart can count the same tile twice.
  • The city may be placed far from the player's start; no travel time or settler cost.
The greedy stub (--planner greedy) is scored with the same objective by model.evaluate, so its score in summary.json compares directly with the MIP's.
